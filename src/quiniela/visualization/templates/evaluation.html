Model evaluation

What this page is for: how good each model is, measured honestly: every past match predicted only with what was known before it (walk-forward). Each group of competitions has its own models. The main metric is the log-loss (lower is better); the hit rate is easier to read. Tickets at the end.

Metrics

Every model on the same matches. Bold: the best model (the market is the reference to beat, not a candidate). ↑ higher is better, ↓ lower is better. ·D and ·R are ways of playing v3, so only their hits and prizes are shown (a double is a hit when the result is one of its signs).

Hit rate by result

For the matches that ended 1, X or 2, how many each model got right. Every model misses most draws: a draw is rarely the most likely result.

Contingency tables

Rows: what happened; columns: what the model said. The diagonal (bold) are the hits; the share under each count is of its row.

Hit rate by confidence

Matches split by the probability of the predicted sign (high, medium, low). If the probabilities can be trusted, each group's hit rate is close to its confidence. Each cell: hit rate and share of matches.

Calibration

Predicted probability (in 10% bins) against how often it happened. On the diagonal: well calibrated; below: over-confident; above: under-confident. Every season of the selected league.

Tickets

The ways of playing the probabilities on the real quiniela tickets of past seasons (those with 10+ La Liga / Segunda matches), with the real prizes of each draw.

Real tickets in euros

Each ticket played on every past draw (·E only when it expects to win), paid with the real prizes and sharing them with the real winners. Back per € below 100% loses money; the quiniela returns 55% on average. ·B is judged by how many 1,000 € prizes it got against how many its plans expected.

How to read the metrics

Formulas of every metric. N matches, pik the probability given to outcome k (1, X, 2) in match i, oik = 1 if that outcome happened, else 0.
